Project: Spot forest traversal (Dreamer-based local adaptation)

Goal:
Teach Spot to traverse a coarse GPS heading (A → Z) through forest terrain using Dreamer as a local adaptive controller. GPS provides only a general direction; Dreamer handles local gait adaptation, obstacle avoidance and robustness without relying on heavy local planners (MPC).

High-level design:
- Hierarchy: coarse GPS heading → Dreamer local controller → low-level PD safety controller.
- Dreamer role: learn a latent world model (RSSM) from multimodal logs and produce imagined rollouts for actor-critic training. The policy outputs gait adjustments or gait-parameter deltas (not raw torques).

World model (our case):
- Inputs: fused camera frames (forward/down), proprioception (joint angles, velocities), IMU, foot-contact, and a short-range goal vector (unit heading toward coarse GPS target).
- Representation: encoder compresses multimodal observations into stochastic latents; RSSM (deterministic recurrent state + stochastic latent) models transitions conditioned on actions (gait parameter deltas).
- Outputs used for training: reconstruction of observations (for representation), predicted rewards, and imagined latent rollouts for training the actor/critic. The model therefore captures both robot dynamics (contacts, slips) and how terrain appears in the camera stream.

Data collection:
- Manually drive Spot through varied forest scenes using built-in gaits; log synchronized streams: timestamps, front/down images, joint states, IMU, contact sensors, commanded gait id/params, GPS heading, and event flags.
- Ensure diversity: different ground (leaf litter, mud, roots), lighting, payloads, speeds, and deliberate near-collision/ recovery maneuvers (safely performed).
- Store episodes as timestamped sequences (e.g., per-episode folders with PNG/NumPy arrays and a metadata manifest).

Training plan:
1. Offline pretrain: train RSSM + reward predictor on collected dataset to learn latent dynamics. Optionally initialize actor via behavior cloning on logged gait adjustments.
2. Actor-critic via imagination: use Dreamer to run imagined trajectories from posterior latents and train actor/critic on λ-returns; regularize to avoid overfitting to logged policy.
3. Deployment + online fine-tune: run actor onboard (low-latency), learner on GPU (remote/nearby) in asynchronous actor-learner mode; allow conservative exploration and continue logging.

Safety & practical notes:
- Use gait-parameter deltas with strict bounds and low-pass filtering (Butterworth) to protect actuators.
- Low-level supervisor must enforce emergency stop, geofence (GPS), tilt/fall detection and fallback to a safe gait.
- Monitor model uncertainty (high KL/reconstruction error) to reduce exploration or switch to fallback behavior.

Evaluation metrics (sample): progress toward heading, collisions/km, falls/km, recovery time, and energy per meter.

This is a concise plan to use Dreamer as the adaptive local controller while relying on coarse GPS headings for global direction. Ready to add a logger template or the Dreamer config next.

ALTERNATIVE FLOW (Recommended for Dutch forests with stumps/branches):
Pretrain RSSM + Online Fine-Tune for Specific Maneuvers

Motivation:
Dutch forests are mostly flat with scattered obstacles (trees, stumps, branches on paths). A fully online DayDreamer from scratch may converge to a general walking policy but struggle with rare, task-specific maneuvers (e.g., stepping over a stump). Pretraining the world model (RSSM) on diverse teleop data gives a reliable latent dynamics foundation; then use DayDreamer online to fine-tune for specific learned behaviors under supervision.

Phase 1: Teleop Data Collection
- Record 2–5 hours of manual Spot drives through the forest.
- Log synchronized: front/down RGB (128×128), IMU (100 Hz), joint angles & velocities, foot contacts, commanded gait id & parameters, GPS heading, timestamps.
- Include deliberate examples: stump crossings, branch avoidance, near-miss recoveries, varied lighting, different payloads.
- Store episodes as folders: PNG images, numpy sensor arrays, JSON metadata (terrain type, incident notes, operator commands).

Phase 2: Offline RSSM Pretraining
- Train encoder + RSSM + decoder + reward head on collected dataset.
- Validation: decode imagined rollouts visually; check reconstruction loss and KL convergence.
- Hyperparams: RSSM hidden 512, latents 32, classes 32, batch size 32, seq len 32, lr 1e-4.
- Outcome: a learned latent dynamics model that reliably predicts terrain and robot state evolution.

Phase 3: Train Baseline Safe-Walking Policy (DayDreamer)
- Use the pretrained RSSM to train actor/critic via imagined rollouts.
- Reward: progress toward heading + alive bonus − heavy collision/fall penalties − small energy penalty.
- Action space: gait-parameter deltas (safer than raw torques).
- Emphasis: strong safety encoding (collision < 0.1 m = severe penalty, fall = severe penalty).
- Output: a robust policy for general walking and obstacle avoidance in the forest.

Phase 4: Runtime Safety Shield (Always Active)
- Proximity veto: if front_min_distance < 0.1 m → stop forward, step-in-place or back-off.
- Action filtering: clip gait-param deltas (|Δ| ≤ 0.02/step), apply Butterworth low-pass filter.
- Tilt/fall detection: if roll/pitch > 45° → cut motors, activate safe recovery gait.
- Human override: kill-switch + remote teleop always available for intervention.

Phase 5: Targeted Online Fine-Tuning (Specific Maneuvers)
- Deploy baseline policy on Spot with asynchronous actor-learner setup (actor onboard, learner on GPU).
- When encountering a specific task (e.g., operator identifies a stump or terrain change):
  - Mark the situation and enable guarded fine-tune mode:
    * Reduce exploration noise, shorten imagination horizon (H ≈ 8–12).
    * Collect targeted episodes of the maneuver (e.g., multiple stump-crossing attempts).
    * Let DayDreamer fine-tune model and policy from those episodes (conservative learning rate).
  - Use curriculum: start with easier variants (small stumps) before harder ones (larger stumps/obstacles).
- Targeted tasks examples: stump crossing, branch ducking, slow over loose terrain, recovery from fall.

Phase 6: Monitoring & Evaluation
- Metrics: collisions/km, falls/km, stump-cross success rate, recovery time after perturbation, forward progress (m/min), energy per meter.
- Model health checks: reconstruction loss, KL, reward-prediction error. If spikes → reduce exploration or pause updates.
- Ablations: with/without vision, pretrained vs online-from-scratch, different imagination horizons.

Key Differences from Pure Online Learning:
- Pretrain RSSM ensures fast convergence of the baseline policy (avoids early random/unsafe behavior).
- Targeted fine-tuning allows efficient learning of rare maneuvers without retraining the entire policy from scratch.
- Safety shield + conservative exploration minimize risky random exploration during online operation.
- Two-stage approach (pretrain general, fine-tune specific) balances robustness and adaptability.

Safety Parameters (suggested):
- Proximity veto threshold: 0.10 m (hard stop), 0.25 m (soft slowdown).
- Max gait-param change per step: 0.02–0.05.
- Imagination horizon (baseline): 12–16 steps; (fine-tune): 8–12 steps.
- Learning rate: 1e-4 (pretrain), 5e-5 (online fine-tune).
- Warmup real steps before heavy updates: 10k.

Next Steps:

2. Train RSSM on the dataset (offline).
3. Train baseline safe-walking policy via DayDreamer.
4. Deploy with safety shield and test in controlled forest section.
5. Fine-tune on specific maneuvers as they arise during field trials.
